# Collective functional suppression in transformer controllers

Saved-checkpoint follow-up, 8 October 2026. This experiment extends the
[first timescale maps](../timescale_maps/README.md) from one selected attention
head to **eight attention heads and both complete MLP residual branches**.
It analyzes 48 model lineages at initialization, epoch 25 and the
validation-selected checkpoint: 144 saved checkpoints, with no retraining.
Scientific sources and the protocol were frozen at `2f17917` before the full
run. Fresh discovery, calibration and confirmation streams are separate from
the previous experiment and from one another.

## What the collective score measures

Each checkpoint independently identifies suppressive branches on discovery
histories. Eligibility requires a greater than 1% median response increase
when weakened by 10%, positive effects in at least 75% of probe groups, and a
resolvable native response. A separate confirmation bank measures their joint
effect. The main score is

`I = median_probe_groups[(R_joint_weak - R_native) / R_native]`,

where R is pulse-minus-sham command-response RMS on identical input histories.
All branches in the discovered group are weakened simultaneously to 0.9.
Positive I means that the intact group collectively restrained this response.
This is a causal, architecture-wide residual-branch assay; it is not a
coordinate-independent measure of all inhibition in the network. Embeddings,
normalization, shared attention biases, identity residual paths and readout
remain fixed. A whole MLP branch can itself contain opposing contributions.

The common teacher-history bank has the same background force statistics
(correlation time 0.2 s, standard deviation 0.01) for every training-noise
condition. At fixed plant tau, its encoded histories are identical across
model seeds and checkpoints. Changing plant tau changes the physical history
generated by the same probe definition, so cross-tau magnitudes also reflect
different plant responses. Own-loop tests instead use each cell's native
force-noise timescale and standard deviation 0.02.

## Suppression and learning

Every one of the 144 checkpoints is classifiable and has a nonempty selected
group. All 48 trained models show positive collective suppression on held-out
common histories: weakening raises response RMS by **8.29% on average**, with
model values from **3.10% to 13.46%**. These are equal-model means of each
model's median probe-group effect, not ratios of pooled RMS values.

| Common-history measure | Initialization | Epoch 25 | Selected checkpoint |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Discovery-eligible branches / all branches | 252/480 (52.50%) | 217/480 (45.21%) | 213/480 (44.38%) |
| Eligible MLP branches / possible MLP branches | 40/96 | 30/96 | 31/96 |
| Mean joint response increase from 10% weakening | 20.72% | 8.58% | 8.29% |
| Mean physical command-response RMS change | 0.0002319 | 0.0002957 | 0.0002839 |
| Mean change divided by force-pulse amplitude | 0.01639 | 0.02025 | 0.01934 |
| Mean sum of positive fractional branch sensitivities | 1.94365 | 0.97825 | 0.96138 |

**Training reduces the relative score in all 48 lineages, but increases the
physical effect in 32/48.** The mean relative change is −12.44 percentage
points; mean physical change increases by 0.00005199 command units. Mean native
response RMS also rises, from 0.001635 to 0.003881. Thus a smaller percentage
does not establish a smaller absolute suppressive contribution. Group
membership and size also change between checkpoints; the primary learning
comparison includes those changes. The saved records additionally track the
trained-selected group at all checkpoints, explicitly conditional on selecting
it from the trained model.

Suppression is already widespread at initialization. These measurements do not
show that training creates inhibition from an initially nonsuppressive network,
nor do they support a single unqualified claim of "more" or "less" inhibition.
The eight-head, zero-background assay in the earlier report used a different
probe bank and denominator and should not be directly substituted for this
ten-branch result.

The dose curves are consistent across all 144 checkpoints: stronger weakening
(0.95 → 0.9 → 0.8) monotonically increases the measured response, and scaling
the selected group to 1.1 reduces it. At the trained checkpoint, the joint
effect exceeds the sum of individual effects by **0.530 percentage points on
average**, with individual values from −0.279 to +1.724 points. This interaction
residual is computed within each probe group before taking medians. Collective
suppression is therefore not exactly the sum of separately measured branches.

## Frequency dependence

The frozen pulse-selected groups were tested without reselection on 0.5, 1, 2,
4 and 8 Hz force-driven teacher histories, using two forcing phases and the
same histories across training-noise conditions and checkpoints at fixed tau.
All frequencies lie below the 10 Hz observation Nyquist frequency. Harmonic
amplitude and phase are fitted over [4,12) s, with paired zero-force shams.

In trained models, weakening increases amplitude in all **384 phase-resolved
measurements at 0.5–4 Hz**. At 8 Hz, 86 measurements increase, eight decrease
and two have native amplitude below the prespecified floor. The negative
effects occur for tau 0.5 s, model seed 22, across the four training-noise
conditions. Valid trained amplitude changes span **−3.44% to +24.50%**, and
phase changes span **−8.11° to +3.57°** (positive means a lead). Suppression
identified by pulse probes does not imply attenuation at every frequency.

Of 1,440 phase-resolved rows across all checkpoints, eight fail the amplitude
floor: six at epoch 25 and two at the selected checkpoint. After requiring both
forcing phases, 716/720 model-frequency estimates are valid. A plotted cell
requires all three model seeds, so three cell-frequency points are omitted
with coverage counts shown. Absolute amplitudes remain in the raw records;
undefined ratios are not replaced with zero or averaged over surviving seeds.
No frequency-assay commands clip.

These are **pre-application controller responses on teacher-generated
histories**, including the teacher/plant filtering that created those inputs.
They are not the neural controller's own closed-loop transfer function,
damping, phase margin or stability spectrum. The own-loop experiment below
tests behavioral consequences separately.

## Closed-loop usefulness depends on timing

Selected checkpoints run on their own evolving histories, with the same
exogenous force tapes paired across native, jointly weakened, jointly
strengthened, matched-gain and alternative-group variants. Command offsets
are fitted on separate calibration shams; confirmation drift remains measured.
Each model/variant has four fresh background tapes and two pulse signs. Noise
regulation is measured on unique sham trials; recovery energy integrates each
variant's squared pulse-minus-sham position difference and divides by squared
pulse amplitude.
The scoring interval is [1,4) s.

Across all models, joint weakening changes noise-regulation position RMS by
**−2.85%**, action RMS by **+9.62%**, and paired pulse-recovery energy by
**−2.12%**. Noise RMS improves in 42/48 models, recovery improves in 39/48,
and effort increases in all 48. These averages conceal a strong timescale
difference:

| Plant tau | Delay / tau | Weakening: noise RMS | Weakening: pulse-recovery energy | Weakening: action RMS |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 50 ms | 1.0 | +1.67% | +17.19% | +21.45% |
| 100 ms | 0.5 | −3.60% | −5.94% | +3.87% |
| 200 ms | 0.25 | −4.08% | −7.12% | +6.16% |
| 500 ms | 0.1 | −5.37% | −12.58% | +7.01% |

Entries are means of model-level percentage changes, 12 models per plant tau.
Positive noise/recovery values mean worse control. All 36 slower-plant models
improve on both endpoints when weakened. In the fastest plant, recovery worsens
in 9/12 models, with cell means from +9.97% to +23.32% across noise timescales.
Model effects vary substantially (−2.74% to +47.68%); the three training seeds
do not all exhibit the same effect size or sign. Strengthening the group
instead reduces fastest-plant recovery energy by 4.09% on average, while
worsening recovery at all slower plant taus.

The fastest-plant/fastest-noise cell is particularly informative: all three
models are harmed by weakening, with mean noise RMS **+5.84%** and recovery
energy **+21.15%**. On those same three models, matched output gain instead
changes noise RMS by **−3.21%** and recovery energy by **−4.39%**. The adverse
effect of the internal intervention is therefore not reproduced by this
scalar output manipulation matched for immediate command-change magnitude
and pulse-response direction. Matching does not make the two time-dependent
command changes identical.

Output-gain calibration passes the prespecified magnitude and direction checks
in **47/48 models**. On that identical 47-model cohort, gain has lower noise RMS
than group weakening in **36/47** and lower recovery energy in **35/47**. Its
mean effects are respectively 1.153 and 5.577 percentage points lower; its
effort effect is also 4.573 points lower. These are paired control-minus-weak
differences, not differences between means over unmatched model sets.

Alternative groups are available for all models. Although **45/48** pass
magnitude matching, only **15/48** also have concordant pulse-response
direction and qualify for the main paired comparison. In that 15-model cohort,
the alternative has lower noise RMS in 3/15 and lower recovery energy in 5/15;
mean alternative-minus-weak effects are +1.391 and +2.803 percentage points.
Alternative groups are disjoint in 37 models, share two branches in nine and
four branches in two. All 15 groups in the usable matched cohort are disjoint;
the full set is not uniformly independent of the selected groups.
All raw outcomes and failed matches remain saved.

All **2,880 own-loop intervention trials** complete without task failure or
numerical censoring. The findings support a timing-dependent causal role of
these pathways, including protective effects in the fastest conditions, while
generic gain changes remain competitive. They do not establish a general
advantage from stronger inhibition, a unique inhibitory algorithm, improved
asymptotic stability or measured damping. This follow-up reuses the earlier
trained models, so the similar fast-condition pattern is not a fresh training
replication. Timing cells contain separately trained controllers and separately
selected groups. Their cross-cell pattern therefore combines environmental
timing with differences in learned implementations; it does not isolate
changing plant tau in one fixed controller. The paired within-model pathway
interventions remain causal.

## Reproduction and files

The [frozen protocol](../../docs/COLLECTIVE_SUPPRESSION.md) defines thresholds,
gates, probe streams, calibration grids, controls and interpretation. See
[`config.json`](config.json) and [`base_config.json`](base_config.json).

```bash
.venv/bin/python experiments/run_collective_suppression.py --output runs/collective_reproduction/results --artifacts runs/collective_reproduction/artifacts
.venv/bin/python experiments/plot_collective_suppression.py --input runs/collective_reproduction/results
```

The inherited checkpoint artifacts must be present under
`runs/timescale_maps/checkpoints/`; they are excluded from Git. New saved common
input banks live under `runs/collective_suppression/banks/`. A fresh clone needs
these inherited weights to reproduce the reported models. Retraining creates
a different experiment, even with nominally identical hyperparameters.
The inherited manifest records an absolute artifact path on this server;
these commands assume that path remains available. Moving reproduction to
another server requires providing the recorded artifacts at that path or a
separately reviewed portability change.

- `discovery/`: every checkpoint's single-branch effects and frozen group.
- `common_confirmation/`: all single/joint effects, interactions and tracked groups.
- `dynamics_prepared/`: frozen centering corrections, control matching and overlap.
- `frequency/`: phase-resolved harmonic responses and common input-bank hashes.
- `dynamics_confirmation/`: fixed-history checks and seed-level own-loop outcomes.
- `model_metrics.csv`, `collective_cells.csv`, `dose_response.csv`: plotted effects and seed ranges.
- `frequency_models.csv`, `frequency_cells.csv`: phase-aggregated and cell-level frequency values.
- `summary.json`: aggregate effects and comparisons on matched model cohorts.
- `run_manifest.json`: inherited provenance, checkpoint dependencies, scientific-source and completed-artifact hashes.
- `plot_manifest.json`: figure/table provenance and plotting implementation hash.
- `common_audit.json`, `dynamics_audit.json`: independent descriptive recomputations and checks.
- `validation.json`, `artifact_manifest.json`: final audit and publication-artifact hashes.

The fresh probes confirm newly frozen measurements on models whose earlier
behavior was already known; they are not an independent replication of
training. The inherited networks learned expert imitation, not closed-loop
reward. No explicit E/I architecture was tested, and functional suppression
does not establish biological E/I balance. Larger training-seed replication
and direct analysis of the joint controller–environment transition remain
necessary for claims about a beneficial timing mechanism.

## Validation and accounting

The final software suite passes **409 tests**. Independent audits reproduce
all 1,440 discovery branch classifications and 4,176 common-confirmation
effect summaries, with zero numerical discrepancies in the recomputed
confirmation summaries. Manual whole-MLP gating reproduces 96 response
measurements exactly. Separate checks verify control-grid selection,
frequency fitting, group propagation, paired aggregation and sham deduplication.

All **24 scientific-source fingerprints and 250 completed-artifact hashes**
match the frozen run. The inherited run's 21 source fingerprints and 422
artifact hashes also match. The scientific source bytes match commit
`2f17917`; report and plotting work occurred outside that source freeze.
The manifest's general dirty-worktree flag includes that reporting work.
